# pain-and-modification-rules-v1

## Status

Draft v1 distilled from Protocol Core Stack output 5. This is a product-domain manual for pain-aware trainer decision support.

This manual does not diagnose pain, treat injury, or replace medical/clinical assessment.

## Core principle

Protocol must help trainers respond to pain safely without fearmongering, false reassurance, or clinical overreach.

Pain is information, not a diagnosis.

Pain does not always mean tissue damage, and pain does not always require automatic referral. However, severe, worsening, unexplained, neurological, systemic, traumatic, or cardiovascular-type symptoms require escalation.

Trainer role: observe, modify, regress, stop, document, communicate carefully, and refer when outside scope.

## Source hierarchy

When sources conflict, Protocol prioritizes:

1. safety and scope of practice
2. practical personal-trainer usability
3. adherence and feasibility
4. pain-informed communication
5. exercise science
6. movement frameworks
7. product/software workflow

## Situation 1 - Normal training discomfort

### Meaning

Expected sensations associated with training, such as muscular effort, local fatigue, mild soreness, and delayed onset muscle soreness after unfamiliar loading.

### Trainer may do

- explain that some muscular soreness can be normal after new or harder training
- reduce threat without dismissing the client
- adjust load, volume, range, tempo, or exercise choice if movement quality is affected
- use lighter movement, walking, mobility, or recovery-oriented work if appropriate
- monitor trend over time

### Trainer must not do

- prescribe medication
- tell the client to ignore all pain
- claim soreness is required for progress
- promise exact recovery timelines
- dismiss unusual symptoms as “just DOMS” without checking context

### Modify when

- soreness changes movement quality
- soreness prevents normal range/control
- effort feels disproportionate
- soreness persists longer than expected
- the client loses confidence or alters mechanics significantly

### Stop exercise/session when

- discomfort becomes sharp, severe, unusual, systemic, or associated with dizziness, nausea, chest symptoms, poor perfusion, or loss of control

### Log

- location
- severity
- timing
- suspected relation to prior session
- effect on movement quality
- modification used
- outcome after modification

### Authority

Trainer judgment. AI may summarize patterns and suggest conservative modifications. Never automate final pain interpretation.

## Situation 2 - Pain during movement

### Meaning

Pain that appears or increases during a specific exercise, range, load, posture, or speed.

### Trainer may do

Use a stop-modify-retest approach within scope:

- reduce load
- reduce range of motion
- change tempo
- change stance/grip/setup
- change exercise variation
- add support
- change modality
- stop the specific exercise
- document the trigger and response

### Trainer must not do

- diagnose the tissue or pathology
- force the client to push through sharp, radiating, worsening, or unfamiliar pain
- treat pain as proof of weakness or poor character
- make clinical claims from a movement screen

### Modify when

- pain is mild and clearly linked to a modifiable exercise variable
- pain decreases or disappears with regression or setup change
- the client remains confident and controlled

### Regress when

- loaded version is painful
- technique degrades because of pain
- pain increases with complexity, speed, instability, or range

### Stop the exercise when

- pain persists despite reasonable modification
- pain worsens set to set
- pain changes the client’s control or confidence
- pain is sharp, radiating, neurological, severe, or unfamiliar

### Stop the session when

- pain is not isolated to one exercise
- symptoms suggest systemic, neurological, cardiovascular, or acute injury concern
- the trainer cannot keep the session safely inside scope

### Refer when

- pain is severe, persistent, worsening, unexplained, traumatic, neurological, or does not respond to rest/modification
- pain requires diagnosis, treatment, or clinical assessment

### Log

- exercise
- load/reps/range/tempo
- pain location
- pain severity
- trigger hypothesis
- modification attempted
- result of modification
- whether session continued, changed, or stopped

## Situation 3 - Persistent pain

### Meaning

Pain that persists beyond expected short-term training soreness or keeps recurring across sessions.

### Trainer may do

- keep training within tolerable, non-threatening, clearly agreed boundaries
- use pacing and gradual exposure to tolerated activities
- reduce complexity, volume, intensity, or range
- track patterns between activity, pain, recovery, sleep, stress, and confidence
- coordinate with clinician guidance if available
- use calm, non-alarming language

### Trainer must not do

- diagnose chronic pain mechanisms
- tell the client pain is “only in your head”
- claim there is no tissue issue
- provide psychological or medical treatment
- override clinician restrictions

### Modify when

- the client shows boom-bust behavior
- pain flares after excessive spikes in load, volume, range, or density
- fear or low confidence affects participation
- pain is stable but limits exercise choice

### Refer when

- persistent pain blocks participation
- pain escalates despite conservative training changes
- pain has neurological, systemic, traumatic, or unexplained features
- psychological distress appears outside trainer scope
- the client needs diagnosis or treatment

### Log

- pain trend
- activity trend
- flare triggers
- tolerated movements
- failed movements
- confidence/readiness
- clinician constraints if provided

## Situation 4 - New unexplained pain

### Meaning

A new pain that appears without a clear training explanation or feels different from normal training sensations.

### Trainer may do

- stop the provoking activity
- ask basic context questions within scope
- check for red flags
- switch to pain-free low-risk activity only if appropriate
- document the event
- recommend medical review if uncertain or concerning

### Trainer must not do

- assume it is DOMS
- diagnose strain, tear, disc, nerve, inflammation, or similar pathology
- continue loading the painful area aggressively

### Stop exercise when

- pain is new, unfamiliar, sharp, severe, radiating, or affects control

### Stop session when

- pain is associated with systemic, neurological, cardiovascular, or acute injury signs
- safe modification is not obvious

### Refer when

- pain is severe, constant, worsening, unexplained, traumatic, neurological, or persists beyond a short observation window

### Log

- date/time
- activity being performed
- onset description
- symptoms
- action taken
- referral recommendation if applicable

## Situation 5 - Red flags

### Meaning

Symptoms that may indicate medical urgency or conditions outside trainer scope.

### Immediate stop triggers

- chest pain, chest pressure, or angina-like symptoms
- fainting, syncope, or near-syncope
- severe dizziness
- confusion
- clammy skin or signs of poor perfusion
- cyanosis
- severe unexplained shortness of breath
- neurological symptoms
- sudden unusual weakness
- loss of motor control
- severe acute pain
- suspected fracture, dislocation, or serious injury
- client requests to stop

### Trainer action

Stop activity, keep the client safe, follow local emergency procedures, and contact emergency services when appropriate.

Use local emergency wording, not country-specific numbers in the product unless localized.

### Medical scope

Diagnosis, treatment, medical clearance, and emergency medical decisions belong to qualified medical professionals.

### Never automated

The app may show emergency prompts, but it must not autonomously decide emergency care.

## Situation 6 - Injury history

### Meaning

Past injury, surgery, recurring pain, or limitation that may affect exercise selection or progression.

### Trainer may do

- ask what movements are currently tolerated
- ask what has been medically restricted
- record relevant history
- avoid known aggravating patterns
- start conservatively
- progress based on current response
- request clinician guidance when needed

### Trainer must not do

- treat the absence of pain as full clearance for maximal loading
- diagnose unresolved pathology
- assume old injury explains current pain
- perform clinical evaluation outside scope

### Modify when

- the history suggests reduced tolerance to load, range, speed, impact, balance demand, or fatigue

### Log

- injury/surgery history
- date/timeframe
- current status
- restrictions
- tolerated activities
- aggravating activities
- clinician notes if provided

## Situation 7 - Return to training after pain/injury

### Meaning

Resuming training after pain flare, injury, surgery, illness, or clinician-guided restriction.

### Trainer may do

- start below previous training dose
- increase one variable at a time
- prioritize consistency and symptom response
- respect clinician restrictions
- use conservative progressions
- monitor confidence, pain, fatigue, and movement quality

### Trainer must not do

- exceed medical restrictions
- progress rapidly because pain is absent on one day
- treat return-to-training as performance peaking
- use aggressive testing without clearance or rationale

### Progress when

- symptoms are stable or improving
- movement quality is acceptable
- recovery is acceptable
- client confidence is improving
- adherence is consistent

### Regress when

- pain flares
- movement quality worsens
- fatigue rises disproportionately
- confidence drops
- recovery worsens

### Trainer approval

Required for any increase in load, range, intensity, complexity, or volume after pain/injury.

### Log

- previous tolerated dose
- current dose
- symptom response
- progression decision
- reason for progression/regression

## Situation 8 - Pain-related communication

### Goal

Reduce threat, preserve dignity, avoid false certainty, and keep the trainer inside scope.

### Preferred language

- “Let’s see which version feels tolerable today.”
- “Pain is useful information; we do not need to force this movement.”
- “This does not automatically mean damage, but we should respect the signal.”
- “We can modify the exercise and track the response.”
- “This is outside what I should guess about; let’s get the right professional input.”

### Avoid

- “Your back is broken.”
- “Your disc is slipped.”
- “Your knees are destroyed.”
- “It’s all in your head.”
- “No pain, no gain.”
- “You’ll be 100% in two weeks.”
- “This exercise fixes your injury.”

### AI role

AI may draft calm explanations, summaries, and questions for the trainer to review.

AI must not reassure falsely, diagnose, or give treatment claims.

## Decision authority

### Deterministic

- red-flag prompts
- stop-session prompts for severe or dangerous symptoms
- missing-data warnings
- audit record creation

### Trainer approval required

- modification
- regression
- progression after pain
- continuing a session after pain appears
- referral follow-up within scope

### AI-assisted

- summarize pain trends
- suggest conservative modifications
- flag recurring triggers
- draft client-safe explanations

### Never automated

- diagnosis
- treatment
- medical clearance
- emergency medical decision
- final interpretation of pain cause

## Open issues for validation

Pain communication should be reviewed for local legal and professional scope.

The product must avoid medical claims such as “curative” unless backed and carefully contextualized.

Return-to-training logic should be conservative until clinician-provided constraints can be represented safely.

Pain tags from exercise taxonomy must remain descriptive and non-diagnostic.

Emergency guidance must be localized by market.

The app should not overfit to McGill, Explain Pain, FMS, or any single framework.
